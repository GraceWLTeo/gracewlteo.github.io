Market Expansion Strategy

Using data to identify opportunities for primary-care expansion

FocusSeminole County, Florida
Service linePrimary care
RoleResearch & Analytics
OutputOpportunity Index

Where should a hospital system expand its primary-care services?

The business question was to identify ZIP codes that warrant further evaluation based on three signals: population and demographics, clinical need, and existing primary-care provider supply. I translated that broad question into an explicit scoring framework and a ranked geographic analysis.

Decision framing: The analysis is a screening tool. A high Opportunity Index indicates an area for further evaluation; it does not, by itself, establish that a clinic should be built there.

Turning an ambiguous question into a model

Demand

Population age distribution was weighted to reflect the analysis's assumed relative demand by age group.

Clinical need

Diabetes, high blood pressure, obesity, and poor-health indicators were combined into a clinical-need score.

Supply scarcity

Primary-care providers per 1,000 residents were normalized and inverted so lower provider density indicates greater scarcity.

Opportunity Index = (Final Demand Score × 0.7) + (Supply Scarcity Score × 0.3)

Data sources

  • U.S. Census — population and age distribution
  • CDC PLACES — population-level chronic-disease and health indicators
  • NPI Registry — primary-care provider counts
  • ZIP-code shapefiles — geographic visualization

How demand was calculated

The model combined an age-weighted population score with a clinical-need score. The weights below are the assumptions used in the original analysis.

Weighted Pop. Age = (0–17 × 0.7) + (18–34 × 0.6) + (35–64 × 1.0) + (65+ × 1.8) Clinical Need = (Diabetes × 0.3) + (High BP × 0.3) + (Obesity × 0.2) + (Poor Health × 0.2) Final Demand = (Weighted Pop. Age × 0.6) + (Clinical Need × 0.4)

From analysis to action

Validate

Compare the screening results with internal claims and utilization data.

Model forward

Incorporate population-growth projections and changing service demand.

Test feasibility

Evaluate payer mix, reimbursement, financial feasibility, and site-level constraints.

What the model does not tell us

  • Provider counts do not capture true clinical capacity, FTEs, or panel sizes.
  • The analysis does not include appointment availability or access constraints.
  • Payer mix, reimbursement, and financial feasibility are outside the model.
  • ZIP-level aggregation can obscure important variation within a ZIP code.

Why this project matters

This project demonstrates a research-to-decision workflow: framing an ambiguous business problem, defining measurable criteria, integrating heterogeneous datasets, making assumptions explicit, building a transparent scoring model, and translating results into a decision-support artifact while identifying the evidence still needed before investment.
