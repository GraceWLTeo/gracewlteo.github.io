Designing a Decision Support Platform

: Helping instructors understand, trust, and act on automated team-performance analytics.

Helping instructors understand, trust, and act on automated team-performance analytics.


Role: Lead UX Designer/Researcher

Methods & Tools: Contextual inquiry, Interviews, Thematic analysis, Journey Mapping, Personas, Measurement framework development, Statistical analysis, AI-based tools

Budget: >$2M

Cross-functional collaborations: Product Manager, System Engineers, Instructors/Subject-Matter-Experts (SMEs).





Goal

  • Support instructors with objective performance insights.
  • Reduce subjectivity in evaluations.
  • Improve training and team performance.

Problem

  • A single instructor is typically responsible for observing and evaluating an entire team's training session.
  • Evaluations are often subjective, inconsistent, and difficult to standardize.

Process

  • Applied design thinking process to ensure the solution was user-centered.

Phase 1: EMPATHIZE - Understanding how Instructors Decide on Evaluations

  • Conducted task analysis, multiple interviews and observations to understand the instructor-user's workflows, information needs, and decision-making processes.

  • From the thematic analysis, the user persona and journey map were developed.
  • These fostered a common understanding of the user throughout the cross-functional team

Phase 2: DEFINE - Synthesize Findings and Identify the Main Problems

Problem #1: Instructors Needed Explanations, Not Just Data

  • Research showed that instructors don't just need data, they also needed to diagnose the causes and explanations of performance issues.
  • For example, location data can show that someone was not at their duty station at a critical time, but communication data can explain why it happened.
  • This led to the first key insight.


Problem #2: Instructors Can't Use Data From Sensors Directly For Evaluations

  • Instructors evaluated the teams on certain evaluation dimensions, e.g., "team communication," "coordination."
  • These dimensions were high-level and cannot be directly observed in the raw data.
  • This led to the second key insight.

Phase 3: IDEATE - Develop Solutions

#1: Time-synchronizing multiple data streams

  • Designed a bird's-eye view displaying team members' locations and head orientation throughout the task.
  • This was accompanied by synchronized communication transcript that provided the context needed to interpret team behaviors.
  • The display aligned location (from GPS), head orientation (from IMUs), and communication transcripts (from audio recordings) on a common timeline.
  • This would help instructors to correlate events and identify contributing factors.


#2: Designing around instructors' mental models

  • The user research identified the behavioral cues used by instructors during team assessment.
  • These cues were converted into measurable behavioral metrics; for example, repeated instructions from a team leader indicated ineffective team communication.
  • These metrics presented the raw sensor data in terms that instructors naturally understood, not simply based on what data were available.
  • This organization of information around the instructors' mental models help make the automated analytics easier to understand and trust.
  • The simple bar-graph style was used so that instructors can easily compare team members' performance.
  • Behavior timestamps linked directly to playback, allowing instructors to review each event in context.


Phase 4: PROTOTYPE

  • An interactive prototype of the decision support platform was developed based on the information needs identified in the journey map.
  • Testers could playback the training session and view where each member was, the direction they were facing, and what was communicated during the session.
  • Testers could also fast-forward, rewind, or jump to any timepoint within the session. Playback speed could be adjusted.
  • There was also a "Behavioral Summary" section where instructors could view when specific behavioral cues were observed.
  • These cues aligned with what they would typically look out for when evaluating the team on the evaluation dimensions.

Data Integration and Trust

  • The platform relied on multiple data sources collected from different sensors and systems.
  • To support diagnosis, these data streams needed to be accurately synchronized on a common timeline.
  • Even small timing discrepancies could lead instructors to draw incorrect conclusions about the relationship between behaviors and outcomes.
  • Working closely with engineers, I identified critical points in the data pipeline where synchronization and integration errors could occur and recommended validation checks to verify data quality before integration.
  • These recommendations were estimated to improve data accuracy by 20–30%.

Research Reflection

  • Research artifacts facilitated shared understanding among the cross-functional product team.
  • User persona and journey map provided a common framework for the team to discuss user needs, pain points, and goals.
  • Measurement framework aligned data collection and system functionality with user needs.
  • Research guided visualization concepts and information architecture
  • Ongoing collaboration with design and engineering teams showed research value throughout every phase of product development.
