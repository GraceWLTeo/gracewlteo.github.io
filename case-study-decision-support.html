Decision Support Platform

Helping instructors evaluate teams with multimodal behavioral data.


Role: Lead Researcher

Methods & Tools: Interviews, Task Analysis, Journey Mapping, Personas, Thematic Analysis, Measurement Framework Development, AI-based tools

Budget: >$2M

Duration: > 2 years

Cross-functional collaborations: Product Nanager, System Engineers, UX Designer, Subject-Matter-Experts (SMEs).

Goal

  • Support instructors with objective performance insights.
  • Reduce subjectivity in evaluations.
  • Improve training and team performance.

Problem

  • A single instructor is typically responsible for observing and evaluating an entire team's training session.
  • Evaluations are often subjective, inconsistent, and difficult to standardize

Process

  • I applied design thinking process to ensure the solution was user-centered.

Phase 1: EMPATHIZE - Understanding how Instructors Decide on Evaluations

  • I conducted task analysis, multiple interviews and observations to understand the instructor-user's workflows, information needs, and decision-making processes.

  • From the thematic analysis, I developed the user persona and journey map.

Phase 2: DEFINE - Core Problem #1

Problem: Instructors Needed Explanations, Not Just Data

  • Research showed that instructors don't just need data, they also needed to diagnose the causes and explanations of performance issues.
  • For example, location data can show that someone was not at their duty station at a critical time, but communication data can explained why it happened.
  • This led to the first key insight.

    Phase 3: IDEATE - Develop solution for core problem #1

  • We designed a display that aligned location (from GPS), head orientation (from IMUs), and communication transcripts (from audio recordings) on a common timeline, enabling instructors to correlate events and identify contributing factors.
  • A bird's-eye view displayed team members' locations and head orientation throughout the task, while a synchronized communication transcript provided the context needed to interpret team behaviors.

Phase 2: DEFINE - Core Problem #2

Problem: Instructors Can't Use Data From Sensors Directly For Evaluations

  • Instructors evaluated the teams on certain evaluation dimensions, e.g., "team communication", "coordination."
  • These dimenssions were high-level cannot be directly observed in the raw data.
  • This led to the second key insight.

    Phase 3: IDEATE - Develop solution for core problem #1

  • Through interviews and instructor notes/handbook, I identified the behavioral cues instructors relied on when making these assessments and developed a measurement framework to operationalize them.
  • For example, repeated instructions from a team leader served as an indicator of ineffective team communication.
  • These behavioral indicators became a critical part of the display since they presented the data in terms of the same behavioral cues instructors naturally used during evaluation.

    Phase 4: PROTOTYPE

    • A lo-fi and interactive decision support platform prototype was developed.
    • Testers could playback the training session and view where each member was, the direction they were facing, and what was communicated during the session.
    • Testers could also fast-forward, rewind, or jump to any timepoint within the session. Playback speed could be adjusted.
    • There was also a "Behavioral Summary" section where instructors could view when specific behavioral cues were observed.
    • These cues aligned with what they would typically look out for when evaluating the team on the evaluation dimensions.

Data Integration and Trust

  • The platform relied on multiple data sources collected from different sensors and systems.
  • To support diagnosis, these data streams needed to be accurately synchronized on a common timeline.
  • Even small timing discrepancies could lead instructors to draw incorrect conclusions about the relationship between behaviors and outcomes.
  • Working closely with engineers, I identified critical points in the data pipeline where synchronization and integration errors could occur and recommended validation checks to verify data quality before integration.
  • These recommendations were estimated to improve data accuracy by 20–30%.

Impact

  • Defined information requirements for instructor workflows
  • Developed a behavioral measurement framework
  • Influenced visualization concepts and information architecture
  • Supported root-cause analysis through synchronized multimodal data views
  • Improved confidence in integrated data through validation recommendations
  • Established a scalable foundation capable of supporting hundreds of instructors
