Designing a Decision Support Platform

: Helping instructors understand, trust, and act on automated team-performance analytics.


Role: Lead UX Designer/Researcher

Methods & Tools: Contextual inquiry, Interviews, Observations, Thematic analysis, Journey Mapping, Personas, Measurement framework development, Statistical analysis, AI-based tools

Budget: >$2M

Cross-functional collaborations: Product Manager, System Engineers, Instructors/Subject-Matter-Experts (SMEs).






Process

  • Applied design thinking process to ensure the solution was user-centered.

Phase 1: EMPATHIZE - Understanding how Instructors Decide on Evaluations

  • Used mixed methods to understand the instructor-users' workflows, information needs, pain points, and decision-making processes.

  • From the thematic analysis, the user persona and journey map were developed.
  • These helped the cross-functional team have a common understanding of the user throughout the platform design process.

Phase 2: DEFINE - Synthesize Findings and Identify the Main Problems

Problem #1: Instructors Needed Explanations, Not Just Data

  • Research showed that instructors don't just need data, they also needed to diagnose the causes and explanations of performance issues.
  • For example, location data can show that someone was not at their duty station at a critical time, but communication data can explain why it happened.
  • This led to the first key insight for the platform design.


Problem #2: Instructors Can't Use Data From Sensors Directly For Evaluations

  • Instructors evaluated the teams on certain evaluation dimensions, e.g., "team communication," "coordination."
  • These dimensions were high-level and cannot be directly observed in the raw data.
  • Instructors look out for certain behavioral cues that are indicative of the evaluation dimensions.
  • This led to the second key insight for the platform design.

Phase 3: IDEATE - Develop Solutions

#1: Time-synchronizing multiple data streams

  • Designed a bird's-eye view displaying team members' locations and head orientation throughout the task.
  • This was accompanied by synchronized communication transcript that provided the context needed to interpret team behaviors.
  • The display aligned location (from GPS), head orientation (from IMUs), and communication transcripts (from audio recordings) on a common timeline.
  • This would give more context and help instructors correlate events and identify contributing factors.


#2: Designing around instructors' mental models

  • User research identified the behavioral cues instructors used to assess team performance.
  • These cues became measurable behavioral metrics aligned with instructor-defined evaluation dimensions.
  • Rather than presenting raw sensor data, the platform organized information around instructors' mental models, making automated analytics easier to understand and trust.
  • Simple bar charts enabled quick comparisons across team members.
  • Each metric linked directly to synchronized playback, allowing instructors to verify the supporting evidence behind every insight.

#3: Data Processing and Integration (Designing for Trust)

  • Utilized AI-assisted transcription (Whisper AI) to speed up the processing of audio communication recordings into timestamped transcripts for behavioral analysis.
  • To support accurate diagnosis, the platform synchronized multiple data streams on a common timeline so instructors could relate behaviors, communications, and events.
  • Because inaccurate synchronization could lead to misleading analytics, I partnered with engineers to identify potential integration failures and define validation checks throughout the data pipeline.
  • These safeguards potentially improved data quality before insights were presented by 20-30%.

Phase 4: PROTOTYPE

  • An interactive prototype of the decision support platform was developed based on the information needs identified in the journey map.
  • Testers could playback the training session and view where each member was, the direction they were facing, and what was communicated during the session.
  • Testers could also fast-forward, rewind, or jump to any timepoint within the session. Playback speed could be adjusted.
  • There was also a "Behavioral Summary" section where instructors could view when specific behavioral cues were observed.
  • These cues aligned with what they would typically look out for when evaluating the team on the evaluation dimensions.

Key Takeaways

  • Research artifacts aligned product, design, and engineering teams around a shared understanding of instructor workflows, goals, and pain points.
  • Personas, journey maps, and the measurement framework guided interaction design, information architecture, and visualization decisions.
  • Behavioral metrics translated complex sensor data into instructor-defined evaluation dimensions, making automated analytics easier to interpret.
  • User trust was supported through transparent design:
    • Synchronized data streams allowed instructors to cross-reference evidence and verify automated insights.
    • Validation checks within the data pipeline improved data quality before analytics were presented.
  • Close collaboration with product, design, and engineering teams ensured user insights shaped decisions throughout the product lifecycle.
